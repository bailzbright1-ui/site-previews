# Industry playbooks

Use this when building for a specific kind of business. Each entry gives:
- **Refs**: the best reference IDs to use as lead or donor. Look at their screenshots.
- **Primary action**: the one thing the site must get people to do.
- **Must-haves**: sections and UX details customers in that industry expect.
- **Fresh angle ideas**: ways to stand out from the typical site in that category.

Mix freely across categories. A plumber built from a fashion reference (with plumber must-haves) is exactly how you get a site that doesn't look like every other plumber.

## Trades & home services
Plumbers, electricians, roofers, builders, painters, cleaners, pest control, pools, landscaping, lawn care, solar, handymen, removalists, fencing, concreting.
- **Refs:** 25 (full conversion layout), 12, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 76, plus 03, 73 and 74 for builders.
- **Primary action:** call now / get a free quote. Put the phone number in the header (tap-to-call on mobile) and a sticky "Get a quote" button.
- **Must-haves:**
  - service list with a short line each
  - service areas / suburbs (good for local SEO)
  - licence and insurance line
  - process steps (3–5)
  - real reviews (Google rating + count)
  - before/after or a project gallery
  - FAQ
  - emergency / after-hours band if relevant
  - quick quote form (name, phone, suburb, service, photo upload optional)
- **Fresh angle ideas:**
  - Atmosphere metaphor (12): show the *result*, like fresh air, a striped lawn or a spotless room.
  - B&W + electric colour (22).
  - Texture edge from the trade (24, paint stroke): grass, timber grain, water.
  - Slash-separated services sentence (21).
  - Tilted sticker for the hero service (20).

## Spec sites for cold outreach (building without being asked)
When building a preview site for a business that hasn't hired you yet:
1. **Use only real, public facts:** business name, services, suburb/service area, phone, hours, existing logo and brand colours, and real public reviews (quote them accurately and attribute them to the platform, e.g. "Google review").
2. **Never invent** testimonials, star ratings, review counts, years in business, licence numbers, awards, team names or stats. Where a fact is unknown, use a clearly marked placeholder (`[Years in business]`) or leave the section out.
3. **Keep their identity.** If they have a logo or colours, the direction picker's *strategy* applies to *their* colours. A preview that looks like a different brand won't convert.
4. **Photos:** use their own photos if they're public and they own them. Otherwise use clearly generic stock that fits the trade, and note it's a placeholder.
5. **Put a small "Preview prepared by [your studio]" note** in the footer so the business knows what it's looking at.
