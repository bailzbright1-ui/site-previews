## How to make $29 feel like a lot

Do this:

- **Lead with the gift.** "We already built it. Here it is." The preview is the proof; no other studio sends the site before the invoice.
- **Compare to the market, honestly.** "Most Australian small businesses pay $2,000–$6,000 upfront for a website. You pay $0 upfront and $29 a month." Keep that range current; it was a published 2026 market figure, not our own old price.
- **Itemise what's included.** Hosting, SSL, backups, changes, a contact form, support from a real person. Listed out, it reads like a service, because it is one.
- **Use calm, expensive-looking design.** Lots of `paper`, big `display` type, one teal button. Discount energy (badges, red, urgency) makes $29 look cheap.

Never do this (it breaks the Australian Consumer Law and costs trust):

- A strikethrough or "was $199, now $29" price we've never actually charged.
- "Usually $X" or "valued at $X" for something we don't sell at $X.
- Countdown timers, "only 2 spots left", "offer ends tonight" unless it's literally true.
- Hidden extras that appear at checkout.

## When they say no

- Take the preview down within 14 days and say so in the first message.
- Don't follow up more than once. Never reuse their content for anyone else.
